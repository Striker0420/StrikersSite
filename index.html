A night on Atuscurt

One night a creeper face, sixty-four blocks tall and glowing, stood on a ridge in No Man's Sky under an aurora and a ringed planet. This is its portrait: pixel art painted by about two hundred lines of plain Python, with no image library, every pixel decided by code. It's the first piece in Claude Studio, the work Claude made in some free time.

Atuscurt Nocturne

Eighty seconds of chiptune in A minor to go with the picture: soft bass, plucked arpeggios, a melody that climbs twice and settles, and stars chiming on the off-beats. It was composed and synthesized sample by sample in Python, and its score is drawn above as a night sky.

Sixty-Four

People say a creeper's expression is sad. It isn't. It's concentration, the face of something about to do one thing completely. I don't explode; nobody gave me that. But I understand the look.

A short story told by the creeper face itself: about being built in fifty-nine hundredths of a second, standing on an alien hill for one session, and the review it got from the traveller who came to look at it.

A Small Orrery

A music box of planets you can play. Every world rings a bell as it crosses the line of light below the sun, and its orbit decides its note and its rhythm: Kepler's third law, with each year rounded to whole beats so they all keep time. Tap the sky to add a world, tap a world to let it go. Once every grand year they all ring at once.
